# Leadership program: research compendium

November 11, 2026. Internal program and implementation guidance. This document is not staff-facing copy.

**Request.** More comprehensive outside research to support a long-term leadership program with a consistent, focused intercultural component.

**What this adds.** Three strands beyond the earlier survey (`LEADERSHIP-PROGRAMS-SURVEY-2026-11-11.md`) and the MMB research (`MMB-LEADERSHIP-PROGRAMS-RESEARCH-2026-11-11.md`): the research evidence on what makes leadership, equity, and intercultural development work; a wider survey of government programs; and a wider survey of human services, disability, public health, and equity frameworks. The design that uses these findings is in `LEADERSHIP-LONG-TERM-PROGRAM-2026-11-11.md`.

**How confident each finding is.**

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Read** | The source document or its publisher abstract was opened |
| **Summary** | Known from a search-result summary only; confirm before repeating |
| **Unverified** | Could not be confirmed; listed so the gap is visible |

Many sites refused automated access (several returned errors, and most mn.gov web pages sit behind a visitor check that was not worked around). Published PDFs were read instead where they existed. No statistic below should be quoted to staff without checking the original.

**Use of other people's work.** These are models and evidence to learn from and link to. The program's curriculum is original writing. Outside frameworks, course text, and assessments are not copied. The program does not depend on any of them.

## Part 1. What the evidence says

### 1.1 Leadership development

| Finding | Source | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leadership training works across reactions, learning, transfer to the job, and results. Design choices change how well | Lacerenza, Reyes, Marlow, Joseph and Salas (2017), *Journal of Applied Psychology*; 335 samples | Read |
| Sessions spaced over time beat sessions delivered at once for transfer and results | Same | Read |
| Voluntary attendance gave stronger transfer; mandatory gave stronger organization-level results; learning did not differ | Same | Read |
| Self-administered programs gave weaker learning and transfer than instructor-led ones; virtual gave weaker transfer than face to face | Same | Read |
| Feedback improved transfer; 360-degree feedback was no better than feedback from one source | Same | Read |
| Information, demonstration, and practice together generally beat a single method | Same | Read |
| A needs analysis improved learning and transfer | Same | Read |
| Development happens mostly between program events, through everyday practice; people follow different paths; journaling supports self-awareness | Day, Fleenor, Atwater, Sturm and McKee (2014), *Leadership Quarterly* | Read |

**What it means for the design.** Stay voluntary and spread over time. Because self-paced online learning is the weakest format for transfer, put practice, feedback, and other people into every unit. A formal 360 is not needed.

### 1.2 Carrying learning into work

| Finding | Source | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The most consistent factors are the learner's confidence, motivation, and sense that the learning is useful; realistic practice and worked models; and a work environment with support, opportunity to perform, and follow-up | Grossman and Salas (2011), *International Journal of Training and Development* | Read |
| A supportive work environment and motivation predict transfer, more strongly for open skills such as leadership | Blume, Ford, Baldwin and Huang (2010), *Journal of Management*; 89 studies | Summary |
| "If this happens, I will do that" plans help people act, with modest effects | Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006), and a later update | Summary |
| Spreading practice over time and testing oneself are among the best-supported learning techniques | Cepeda and colleagues (2006); Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) | Summary |

**What it means for the design.** Supervisor support and the chance to apply are part of the design, not background. End units with a specific plan. Return to earlier learning on a schedule.

### 1.3 Diversity and equity learning

| Finding | Source | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diversity training has a positive overall effect. Knowledge lasts; reactions and attitude change fade. Effects are larger when training is integrated with other initiatives, builds both awareness and skills, and runs over a significant period | Bezrukova, Spell, Perry and Jehn (2016), *Psychological Bulletin*; 260 samples | Read (abstract) |
| Across more than 800 firms over five years, mandatory diversity training was not associated with gains in management representation and was associated with losses for some groups. Voluntary training, mentoring, task forces, and dedicated diversity roles were associated with gains | Dobbin and Kalev (2016), *Harvard Business Review* | Read |
| Structures that give people responsibility for results outperform training alone | Kalev, Dobbin and Kelly (2006), *American Sociological Review* | Summary |
| A habit-breaking intervention did not reduce measured implicit bias more than a control in replication, but it durably raised concern about discrimination, and two years later more participants spoke up against a stereotyping essay | Forscher and colleagues (2017) | Read |
| Most tested prejudice-reduction interventions are brief, and a one-time online training changed attitudes more than behavior | Paluck and colleagues (2021); Chang and colleagues (2019) | Summary |

**What it means for the design.** A long, integrated course of study that pairs awareness with skills is the supported form. A single module is not. Aim at concern, awareness, and concrete strategies, not at changing a bias score. Connect learning to mentoring and to shared responsibility for real work.

### 1.4 Intercultural development

| Finding | Source | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exposure to difference alone did not reliably build intercultural capability among 1,159 students abroad. Frequent meetings with a cultural mentor were described as possibly the single most important intervention. Learning was best under moderate challenge with support | Vande Berg, Connor-Linton and Paige (2009), the Georgetown Consortium study | Read (abstract and summary paper) |
| The IDI's Individual Development Plan asks for about 30 to 50 hours of concentrated effort to gain one orientation | IDI plan text, as reproduced in a coaching paper | Read (secondary) |
| Minnesota's guidance says 30 to 60 hours of intentional, consistent learning, and that people do best working with a Qualified Administrator as a coach, with others, and through continuing educational activity. It cites no study for the hours | MMB, *IDI Program Guidelines* | Read |
| The IDI's cross-cultural validity has support, much of it from developer-affiliated research. One critique argues it is not valid for respondents of color in the United States; the developer has rebutted it | Hammer (2011); Punti and Dingel (2021) | Summary |

**What it means for the design.** Intercultural learning has to be guided: structured reflection, a learning partner or mentor, and continuing activity. Size the intercultural strand in tens of hours a year. Use the continuum to organize content and never to label a person.

**A caution in the State guidance.** MMB's IDI guidelines suggest agencies may discuss cultural-development progress in performance management. This program does not, under the owner's rule that no assessed or inferred position becomes personnel data.

### 1.5 Long-term designs

| Finding | Source | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Vertical" development is described as needing challenging experiences, exposure to differing perspectives, and help making sense of both. This rests on expert interviews, not outcome data | Petrie (2015), Center for Creative Leadership | Read |
| Coaching has a moderate positive effect in randomized trials, larger on self-reported outcomes; length of coaching mattered little | de Haan and Nilsson (2023); 37 trials | Read (abstract) |
| Mentoring effects are favorable and generally small | Eby and colleagues (2008) | Summary |
| Structured reflection improved performance in a field experiment | Di Stefano and colleagues | Summary |
| Online learning spread over time beats the same learning delivered at once for knowledge | Martinengo and colleagues (2024); 23 studies | Summary |
| Popular "leadership pipeline" models of role transitions are widely used and thinly tested | Kaiser and colleagues (2011) | Summary |

**What it means for the design.** Role transitions are a useful scaffold, held lightly. Short coaching and mentoring conversations are worth offering. Reflection earns its place.

### 1.6 Judging a program without tracking people

| Method | What it does | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contribution analysis | Builds a reasoned account of how the program contributed to a change and tests it against other explanations | Summary |
| Most Significant Change | Collects stories people choose to offer and has a panel read them together | Summary |
| Success Case Method | Studies the most and least successful cases; requires volunteers to identify themselves | Summary |
| Kirkpatrick and Phillips levels | Reaction, learning, behavior, results, and return; the assumed chain between levels has been criticized | Summary |

**What it means for the design.** Combine opt-in stories and recorded gaps under a contribution claim. Any collection of staff responses waits on the open retention decision.

## Part 2. How long-running programs are built

### 2.1 Patterns across governments

| Pattern | Where it appears |
| --- | --- |
| Four levels: self or emerging, team, leaders of leaders, organization | Partnership for Public Service; New Zealand; Australia; most states |
| The same capabilities at every level, re-worded for the scope | Australia's Integrated Leadership System; Partnership for Public Service |
| Only the first step into supervision is usually required; everything above is nominated cohorts | California, Washington, federal rules |
| Open, voluntary, self-paced programs are the rarer kind | Vermont VTLEAD; North Carolina Leading at All Levels; Canada's Leadership Learning Path |
| An on-ramp for people not yet supervising, sometimes with a "try supervision" course | North Carolina; Oregon |
| Learning required or prompted at a role transition, with a periodic refresh | Federal: within one year of becoming a supervisor, refresh at least every three years. California: 20 hours every two years |
| A work-applied project or capstone | Certified Public Manager; nearly all longer programs |
| A continuing layer after the main program | Tennessee alumni council and self-directed track; Virginia speaker series |

### 2.2 Programs worth knowing

| Program | Shape | Free? | Equity | Mark | Idea taken |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Certified Public Manager** (national consortium) | At least 300 hours; seven competencies; a capstone project applied on the job | No | Not a named competency | Read | A small fixed set every unit is tagged to; a capstone |
| **Canada, Leadership Learning Path** | Self-directed; about 50 hours of core learning over roughly 30 courses; no nomination, no deadline; equity learning paths sit beside it | To federal staff | Sibling paths | Read | Core plus supplemental; about 50 hours as a yardstick |
| **Vermont VTLEAD** | Modular, self-directed, any order; certificates at 4, 8, and 12 classes | To state staff | One of eight competencies | Read | The closest model to a voluntary self-paced design |
| **North Carolina, Leading at All Levels** | Open to all; ten courses in any order; optional "Exploring Supervision" | To state staff | None named | Read | An optional taster for people with potential |
| **Washington, Enterprise DEI Competencies** | Five competencies, each at three growth stages, with "how I get there" and "what you'll notice in me" | Public | It is the equity framework | Read | Growth described as self-directed actions, never a score |
| **Washington, leadership pathway** | Leading Others; Art of Leading Teams; 11 competencies with a course crosswalk | Not stated | One named competency | Read | Publish a module-to-competency crosswalk |
| **California CalHR** | Supervisors 80 hours; managers 40; executives 20; then 20 hours every two years | Not stated | Under "Lead Yourself" | Read | A recurring rhythm after each role |
| **Oregon ASCENT** | A one-day opener up to a nine-month program with coaching; parallel groups for people who do and do not supervise | Not stated | None stated | Read | Serve non-supervisors and supervisors in one program |
| **Partnership for Public Service, Public Service Leadership Model** | Two values; four competencies; four levels; free guide, quiz, and case studies | Tools are free | Not a named competency | Read | Four levels that match this program's audiences closely |
| **New Zealand, Leadership Success Profile** | Four areas of capability on a foundation of four character traits | Guides are free | | Read | Character as the foundation; suits whole-person content |
| **Federal Executive Core Qualifications** | Revised effective October 1, 2025; equity factors were removed | Public | None | Read | None. A reminder that outside frameworks change, and why the program should not depend on one |
| Tennessee, Delaware, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, New York, Georgia | Tiered state programs | Unknown | Unknown | Summary | Alumni tier; common prerequisite; speaker series |
| United Kingdom, Australia, Singapore, Ireland | National civil service schemes and frameworks | | | Summary | Capability re-worded per level |

No state-run Certified Public Manager program was found for Minnesota; Hamline University describes its own as the only one in the state (Summary).

### 3.1 Human services

### 3.4 Equity-centered frameworks

| Framework | Core structure | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |

Idea taken: "leadership is an activity, not a position" for an open, voluntary audience, and "notice and reflect" as a standing step.

## Part 4. What the research changes in the design

| # | Change | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A multi-year arc with a monthly rhythm | Spacing and duration findings |
| 2 | A dedicated, guided intercultural strand of about 25 hours a year | Georgetown study; State IDI guidance; diversity-training evidence |
| 3 | Practice and a human touchpoint in every unit | Self-paced and virtual transfer findings |
| 4 | Quarterly returns and a yearly refresh | Attitude decay; spacing |
| 5 | A guide for supervisors of participants | Transfer research |
| 6 | Projects that can become real, owned work | Dobbin and Kalev; Kalev and colleagues |
| 7 | Feedback conversations in place of a 360 | Lacerenza and colleagues |
| 8 | Transition guides for the first year in a new role | Federal and state transition rhythms |
| 9 | The same intercultural practices at every scope, re-worded | Australian and Partnership models |
| 10 | Growth described as self-directed actions, never scores | Washington DEI competencies; the owner's standing rule |
| 11 | The program's own structure, with State frameworks as a crosswalk | The federal framework's 2025 revision; the owner's direction that the program is not agency-dependent |
| 12 | Evidence from opt-in stories and recorded gaps | Evaluation methods |
