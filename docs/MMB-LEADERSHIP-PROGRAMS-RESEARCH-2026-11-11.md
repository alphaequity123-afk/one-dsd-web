# MMB Emerging Leaders Institute and the State's leadership framework: research

November 11, 2026. Internal program and implementation guidance. This document is not staff-facing copy.

**Request.** The owner asked for research into how Minnesota Management and Budget's Emerging Leaders Institute is structured, so that structure can inform the One DSD leadership program and the owner's statewide work.

**Scope.** Public MMB sources only. No internal DHS or MMB material was requested or read, and no one was contacted. This extends the September 8 public-source analysis in `EMERGING-LEADERS-DSD-ANALYSIS-2026-09-08.md`; it does not reopen the closed request for internal Office of Employee Culture materials.

**How it was gathered, and what that limits.** MMB's published PDFs were downloaded and read in full. MMB's web pages (the course catalog, the application page, the Core programs page, and the Leadership Learning Hub site) sit behind an automated-visitor check and could not be opened directly. One catalog page was read successfully; other page facts come from search-result summaries and are marked "search summary" below. No session-by-session ELI curriculum is published in any source found.

---

## 1. Bottom line

1. **The most useful finding is not ELI itself. It is the State's Enterprise-Wide Leader Competency Model**, which ELI and every other MMB leadership program are organized around. It is public, it is explicitly equity-centered, and it gives the One DSD program a statewide frame to align with.
2. **ELI's structure is public; its curriculum is not.** The cohort design, schedule, learning methods, eligibility, application questions, and selection process are published. The topics taught in each session are not.
3. **ELI is competitive and gated.** It needs two years of state service, a leader's recommendation, HR director approval, and selection by committee. A self-paced DSD program can serve the people ELI cannot yet reach and help them prepare for it.
4. **MMB already runs a site called the Leadership Learning Hub.** The One DSD program needs a name that will not be confused with it.

## 2. How ELI is structured

| Element | What the public sources say | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | A cohort-based program "designed to prepare future leaders in Minnesota government." Builds knowledge of public sector leadership and individual leadership effectiveness "through critical thinking, interviews, study, and reflection" | 2026 application information |
| Length and rhythm | Seven months, September to March, twice a month. 13 sessions per cohort | 2026 application information |
| Session pattern | Alternates shorter days (8:30 a.m. to 1:45 p.m.) with full days (8:30 a.m. to 4 p.m.). Opens with a ceremony and closes with a full day of final presentations | 2026 application information |
| Delivery | Three cohorts for 2026-2027: one hybrid (Tuesdays), one in person (Wednesdays), one virtual (Fridays). Opening and graduation in person at the Capitol with a livestream | 2026 application information |
| Cohort size | "Three groups of 36 participants" each September. A 2019 union article gave 72 in total, so the size has grown | ETD catalog page; MAPE article |
| Learning methods | Instruction from topical experts; small-group interaction; interviews with other leaders; 360-degree feedback "designed to build self-awareness in leadership context"; assignments between sessions; final presentations | 2026 application information |
| Competencies | Getting Work Done, Leading Others, Leading Self, Leading the Organization, Leading with DEAI | ETD catalog page |
| Skills | Creative thinking, interpersonal, leadership, strategic thinking | ETD catalog page |
| Eligibility | Two years of state employment by the start date (24 months, not necessarily consecutive); support from the supervisor or chain of leadership; sign-off by the agency HR director; commitment to attend every class and complete every assignment | 2026 application information |
| Application | Each agency runs its own internal process and may set an earlier deadline and extra requirements. A fillable form, a letter of recommendation on agency letterhead, and an HR letter of approval | 2026 application information; ELI and SLI process guide |
| Selection | "Highly competitive." Hundreds apply to ELI and SLI each year; up to two review committees; decisions in early August | ELI and SLI process guide |
| Cost | $3,840, with no refund for withdrawal (search summary of the application page; confirm with MMB before repeating) | Search summary; 2025 application form |
| 360 instrument | A vendor evaluation on a State procurement site names the Zenger Folkman Extraordinary Leader report with a facilitated debrief. A single secondary source; treat as unconfirmed | Search summary |

### What the application asks

The 2025 form asks each applicant, in 1,500 characters each:

1. What qualities are important in future leaders in public service?
2. How do you exhibit these qualities in your current role?
3. What are your future aspirations or goals as a leader?
4. What skills do you hope to develop?
5. In what ways have you demonstrated a commitment to supporting or furthering DEAI initiatives at work, and how have you sought to increase your own self-awareness and growth in these areas?
6. How will participating benefit your agency and further the State's goals?

The recommendation letter is asked to describe leadership characteristics, and the first example listed is "Values accessibility, inclusion, equity, and diverse perspectives." Others include working collaboratively, accepting and using feedback, and continually seeking personal and professional growth.

## 3. Where ELI sits among the State's programs

| Program | For | Shape | Entry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic Effectiveness for Aspiring Leaders (SEAL) | Employees in non-management roles who aspire to lead | Inclusive leadership, persuasive presentations, running meetings, development planning. Length and cost not found | Enrollment; no application mentioned |
| Emerging Leaders Institute (ELI) | Future leaders | Seven-month cohort, as above | Competitive application |
| Supervisor Core and Manager Core | New supervisors and managers | Required for job certification under Administrative Procedure 21, within the probationary period. Manager Core: six full days, $925 (search summary) | Required by appointment |
| Senior Leadership Institute (SLI) | Experienced managers and directors preparing for higher roles | Four months, with the University of Minnesota Humphrey School of Public Affairs. Adds the competency Leading Outside the Organization | Competitive application; letter from a deputy commissioner or similar |

Every program above is tagged to the same competency set.

## 4. The Enterprise-Wide Leader Competency Model

Source: *Competency Definition Guide: Developing Leader Competencies for State of Minnesota Employees*, MMB, March 2024.

The guide says the model was built by Enterprise Talent Development with learning, HR, and DEAI leaders to "set expectations, better identify, and develop our existing and emerging state leaders."

**Its equity-centered guiding statement:** the model equips leaders to identify, select, promote, engage, and retain employees "through accessible, anti-racist, and equity-centered approaches." It lists six acknowledgments, including that oppressive systems exist within the State's own structures and that leaders are responsible for removing barriers to diversity, equity, accessibility, and inclusion.

| Competency | Definition in the guide | Equity content written into it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leading Self | "A commitment to personal and professional growth through self-reflection and self-improvement" | Cultural humility and identity are key skills. Leaders are aware of their social identities, learn about personal biases, and take "personal responsibility for diversity and inclusion outcomes" |
| Leading Others | "Develop interpersonal skills and influence as you work alongside your leadership partners and guide immediate teams" | Intercultural communication and intersectionality are key skills. Expert behavior includes "disrupts organizational norms and patterns that perpetuate inequities" |
| Leading the Organization | "Guide and set vision with a large-scale team or group of teams while maintaining accountability, transparency, and integrity" | Allyship and advocacy are key skills. Proficient behavior includes applying a DEI framework to leadership behaviors |
| Leading Outside the Organization | Collaborating with partners beyond one's department, agency, or the enterprise | Anti-racism is a key skill. Expert behavior includes engaging community before developing solutions and changing policies that keep people from using services |
| Getting Work Done | "Completing daily tasks and following procedures with a high level of efficiency" | Expert behavior includes evaluating projects "using an equity lens" and calling out racism, ableism, and other inequities |

The ETD catalog also tags programs with a sixth label, **Leading with DEAI**, which the March 2024 guide does not define as its own chapter.

Each competency has three behavior levels: **in-progress, proficient, and expert**. The guide offers them for reflecting on "how well you and your teams have adapted or implemented these skills."

**An observation of ours, not a claim MMB makes.** The Leading Self levels track the program's own theory of change. In-progress includes "recognizes cultural differences but overemphasizes commonalities," which is how Minimization is described. Proficient includes recognizing "culturally different ways of making sense of and responding to cultural differences," which resembles Acceptance. Expert includes "shifts their cultural perspective and adapts behavior to different cultural contexts," which resembles Adaptation. The State's competency model and the IDI continuum can be taught side by side without strain.

**The Leadership Learning Hub.** MMB runs an online resource library of that name at `mn.gov/mmb/leadership-learning-hub`, organized by the same competencies, for employees at every level. Its pages could not be opened for this research.

## 5. What to carry into the One DSD program

| From MMB | How the One DSD program could use it | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The competency model | Make it the shared frame. Show, for each pathway and lesson, which State competency it builds, so DSD learning speaks the same language as ELI, the Core programs, and SLI | Cite the guide as the State's source; do not reproduce it wholesale. Leading Outside the Organization names tribal governments, and any content there defers to the Office of Indian Affairs and the DHS tribal offices |
| The three behavior levels | Offer them as private reflection language: "which of these sounds like me this month?" | Never stored, scored, or shown to anyone else. No level becomes a label on a person |
| Seven months, twice a month | Offer an optional pacing guide in the same rhythm for learners who want structure: one shorter and one longer touchpoint each month | A planning pattern only. No deadlines |
| Expert instruction | Short lessons from the existing course collection | Already in the outline |
| Small-group interaction | The Learning Lab and Amplify, where a learner chooses to join | No assigned groups |
| Interviews with other leaders | A leader-interview guide: how to ask an experienced leader how they handled an equity tradeoff, and what to do with the answer | The learner arranges the conversation. The program contacts no one |
| 360 feedback | The feedback conversation guide already on the leadership page | No 360 instrument. ELI's is licensed and professionally debriefed |
| Final presentations | An optional closing summary the learner writes and may choose to share with a supervisor or at a Learning Lab | Not required; not assessed |
| The six application questions | Use them as opening reflection prompts in the first pathway. A learner who later applies to ELI will have thought through every question | Say plainly that this prepares a person to apply; it does not improve their odds or replace the agency process |
| The gap ELI leaves | State the program's distinct contribution: open now, without two years of service, a recommendation, selection, or tuition, and centered on equity practice in disability services work | Describe ELI as a complementary formal opportunity. Never suggest equivalence or credit |

### How the five pathways line up

| One DSD pathway | State competencies emphasized | Complementary State program |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Leading yourself (staff with leadership potential) | Leading Self; Getting Work Done | SEAL; ELI once eligible |
| 2. Leading a program (program leads) | Leading Others; Getting Work Done; Leading Outside the Organization | ELI |
| 3. Leading a team (supervisors) | Leading Others; Leading Self | Supervisor Core (required separately); ELI |
| 4. Leading across teams (managers, deputy and assistant directors) | Leading the Organization; Leading Others | Manager Core (required separately); SLI |
| 5. Leading the division (directors) | Leading the Organization; Leading Outside the Organization | SLI |

Leading with DEAI runs through all five.

## 6. Decisions for the owner

| # | Decision | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Adopt the State competency model as the program's shared frame, alongside the IDI continuum and the seven DEIA capabilities | Yes. It is the State's own equity-centered standard and makes the program portable beyond DSD |
| 2 | The program's name, given MMB's Leadership Learning Hub | Use "One DSD Leadership Journey," from the owner's own draft title |
| 3 | Offer the optional seven-month pacing guide | Yes, clearly marked as optional |
| 4 | Add the ELI application questions as opening reflections, with a short "considering ELI?" note | Yes |
| 5 | Whether to ask MMB Enterprise Talent Development for the ELI session topics | The owner's call and the owner's contact to make. It is the one thing public sources do not show |
