## 1. The program in one page

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it is** | A voluntary, online, self-paced leadership development program in which equity and inclusion governs how every leadership topic is taught, practiced, and reflected on |
| **Who it is for** | Staff identified with leadership potential, and new or experienced program leads, supervisors, managers, and directors |
| **Purpose** | To equip them with advisory and practical knowledge, insight, and skill, professional and personal, to lead with an equity mindset and lens |
| **Theory of change** | The IDI continuum. The program helps leaders move their own practice, their teams, and their systems from Minimization toward Acceptance, Adaptation, and Integration |
| **Shape** | A six-module common core, then five pathways of six modules each, by scope of leadership work. 36 modules of 10 to 20 minutes, each ending in real-work application and reflection |
| **How people learn** | Learn, reflect, apply, capture, return. Most of the learning happens in the participant's own work |
| **With others** | Optional conversations with a peer, mentor, sponsor, experienced leader, or learning group, arranged by the participant |
| **Record** | A private journal and portfolio on the participant's own device, which only they can share |
| **What it is not** | A cohort, a nomination scheme, a credential, a performance measure, required training, or a replacement for the State's formal leadership programs |

**The promise to a participant.** You can start today, in any role, without anyone's permission. What you write is yours. Nothing here rates you. You will finish each module having tried something in your real work and looked honestly at what happened.

## 2. Foundations

### 2.1 Theory of change

The Intercultural Development Inventory continuum is the program's direction of travel: Denial, Polarization, Minimization, Acceptance, Adaptation, Integration.

| Orientation | How it shows up in leadership practice |
| --- | --- |
| Denial | Difference is not noticed. "We don't have that issue here." |
| Polarization | Difference is noticed and judged. "Our way" against "their way," in either direction |
| Minimization | Commonality is stressed and difference is smoothed over. "I treat everyone the same." The leader's own default passes as neutral |
| Acceptance | Difference is recognized as real and meaningful, including one's own culture as one among many |
| Adaptation | The leader changes behavior, process, and policy so that they work across difference |
| Integration | Equity is how the unit works, and it holds when leaders change |

The continuum describes practices, teams, and systems, and gives each participant a language for their own reflection. The program never places a person on it. Licensed assessment stays separate and professionally administered.

### 2.2 The equity frame: five questions

Every leadership topic is taught through the same five questions. They are the five shared learning focuses the applications already use.

| Focus | The leader's question | What it guards against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity | What am I treating as the normal way, and where did I learn it? | One's own default passing as neutral |
| Perspectives | Who is affected, whose experience is missing, and how would I find out? | Deciding for people without them |
| Communication | How do I adapt so people can take part, and how will I check it worked? | Access offered in one form only |
| Decisions | Who decides, what evidence do we have, and what does an equity analysis show? | Good intentions without changed decisions |
| Reflection | What happened, for whom, and what will I keep, change, or stop? | Counting activity as results |

**Mindset and lens.** The first two questions build the equity mindset: the personal, inward work. The third and fourth build the equity lens: the professional method applied to a decision. The fifth joins them.

### 2.3 The leadership skeleton

The State's Enterprise-Wide Leader Competency Model supplies the leadership topics, so the program speaks the same language as the State's own leadership programs.

| State competency | In brief |
| --- | --- |
| Leading Self | Growth through self-reflection, cultural humility, awareness of identity and bias |
| Leading Others | Building inclusive teams, coaching, feedback, conflict, equitable opportunity |
| Leading the Organization | Vision, strategy, change, and resourcing across teams |
| Leading Outside the Organization | Partnership, community engagement, accountability to the public |
| Getting Work Done | Delivering results with an equity lens on projects and processes |

### 2.4 Whole-person purpose

Helping a participant examine their own culture and defaults is as much the program's job as building their skill in serving a particular community or running a team. This applies with particular force to dominant-culture staff examining assumptions they have not had to notice. Introspective modules are never marked optional relative to operational ones.

### 2.5 Calling in

The program welcomes questions and examines evidence, decisions, and practices. It does not shame, and it does not infer anyone's motives from their identity.

### 2.6 Learning design principles

| Principle | Consequence for the design |
| --- | --- |
| Adults learn from their own work | Every module ends in an application the participant chooses and sizes |
| Formal learning should be small | Lessons run 10 to 20 minutes |
| Reflection turns experience into learning | Reflection is built into every module and returns a week and a month later |
| Relationships deepen learning but cannot be forced | Conversations are optional and arranged by the participant |
| Learning is a cycle | "Return" is an invitation, with no completion ladder |
| Trust requires privacy | Nothing is recorded about a person; the participant controls every share |

The 70-20-10 proportion (experience, relationships, formal learning) guides authors. It is a rule of thumb and is not shown to staff as a requirement.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Three layers

| Layer | Content | Stays the same everywhere? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Frame** | The five questions, the continuum, the State Equity Analysis Tool | Yes |
| **Skeleton** | The five State leader competencies, arranged as a core and five pathways | Yes |
| **Setting** | The unit's own work: its services, the people it serves, its decisions, its scenarios | No. Each unit supplies its own |

The frame and skeleton are the program. The setting is what makes it land in a particular unit's work.

### 3.2 How a conventional topic is reworked

| Step | What the author does |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Take a conventional leadership topic |
| 2 | Tag it to a State competency |
| 3 | Ask the five questions of it |
| 4 | Describe what it looks like at Minimization, Acceptance, and Adaptation |
| 5 | Place it in a real decision from the setting |
| 6 | Give an application the participant can size to their role |

### 3.3 Lineage

| Element | Model it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Competency skeleton; equity written into every competency | MMB competency model |
| Rings from self outward; tracks by role | MMB, federal, and child welfare leadership models |
| Twice-monthly rhythm; leader interviews; feedback; closing presentation | MMB Emerging Leaders Institute |
| Equity placed first; begin with discovery in one's own context | Center for Creative Leadership, REAL |
| See, then engage, then act | National Equity Project, Leading for Equity |
| Tie leadership to a result for a population | Annie E. Casey Foundation, Results Count |
| Operationalize equity in decisions | State of Minnesota Equity Analysis Tool; Government Alliance on Race and Equity |
| Experience first | 70-20-10 |
| Perspective of people served | Minnesota Partners in Policymaking, for disability settings |

All of these are referenced and linked. The curriculum is original program writing.

## 4. Who it is for

### 4.1 Five audiences, five pathways

| Pathway | For | Scope of leadership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Leading yourself | Staff identified with leadership potential | One's own practice and influence |
| 2. Leading a program | Program leads | Work guided without supervisory authority |
| 3. Leading a team | Supervisors | A team of direct reports |
| 4. Leading across teams | Managers, including deputy and assistant directors | Several teams, resources, and priorities |
| 5. Leading the organization | Directors | The unit's direction, culture, and continuity |

### 4.2 New and experienced

Each pathway module has two lessons. **Start here** assumes the participant is new to that scope of work. **Go further** assumes they have done it for some time and are ready to examine their established habits. The participant chooses; nothing labels them.

### 4.3 "Identified with leadership potential"

Identification is an invitation. A person can step forward themselves, or a colleague or supervisor can encourage them. The program keeps no list and does not check. This is the recommended reading and remains the owner's decision (section 21).

### 4.4 Open access

Any participant can open any pathway, mix them, and stop at any point. There are no prerequisites, levels, or minimum service.

## 5. What participants gain

### 5.1 Four strands

| Strand | Meaning | Where it is built |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal insight | The equity mindset: one's own culture, defaults, and place in the work | Core modules 2 and 3; the curiosity question in every module; the journal |
| Professional knowledge | What a leader at this scope needs to know about people, systems, policy, and the setting | Lessons; original policy sources; the setting's own content |
| Practical skill | The equity lens applied to real decisions | Applications; the planner; scenarios |
| Advisory capability | Advising well and using advice well | Options briefs; coaching and sponsorship modules; conversation guides |

### 5.2 Program outcomes

A participant who works through the core and a pathway should be able to:

1. Name a default of their own that they had treated as neutral, and what changed once they saw it.
2. Describe the six orientations as they appear in a team's ordinary practice.
3. Apply the five questions to a decision from their own work.
4. Work a decision through the State Equity Analysis Tool and say who decides.
5. Adapt how they communicate, meet, or delegate so more people can take part, and check that it helped.
6. Distinguish what was delivered, what was applied, and what benefit was observed, and state the limits of the evidence.
7. Prepare or use an options brief that shows tradeoffs and who bears them.
8. Describe one change they made in real work, what happened, and what they would do next.

These describe what the program teaches toward. They are not tested, scored, or recorded.

### 5.3 Outcomes by pathway

| Pathway | The participant leaves able to |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | State a leadership purpose that names who it is for; hold a development conversation; make one small change that widens access |
| 2 | Map where a process loses people; identify the decision owner; bring affected perspectives into an options brief |
| 3 | Tie hiring criteria to duties; give feedback about the work; make opportunity visible; hold a stay conversation and respond |
| 4 | Show who a priority or a cut lands on; plan a change with a report-back; build access in instead of waiting to be asked |
| 5 | Compare stated values with what is rewarded; take a barrier through its official route; review continuity without naming an heir |

## 6. The learning cycle

| Stage | What the participant does | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Learn** | One short lesson: an idea, a scenario from the setting, and an example | 10 to 20 minutes |
| **Reflect** | What? So what? Now what? in the journal | 5 to 10 minutes |
| **Apply** | Try one thing in real work, sized to their role | Within one to two weeks |
| **Capture** | Note what was tried, what happened, and what the evidence does and does not show | 5 to 10 minutes |
| **Return** | Come back at about a week and a month; keep, change, or stop; choose what is next | 5 minutes each |

Times are estimates and have not been tested with staff.

## 9. The five pathways

Each module lists the conventional topic, the equity question that reworks it, the central outcome, and an application. Existing content that can anchor each module is listed in the curriculum document.

### 9.1 Pathway 1: Leading yourself

**For:** staff identified with leadership potential. **Competencies:** Leading Self; Getting Work Done.
**New to this:** discovering that leadership is a practice available in any role. **Experienced:** examining habits that have worked for you and who they work less well for.

| # | Topic | Equity question | The participant can | Application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | Purpose and values | Whose interests does my idea of a good leader serve? | State a leadership purpose that names who it is for | A purpose statement |
| 1.2 | Self-awareness | When am I least curious, and about whom? | Separate what they observed from what they assumed | A critical-moment reflection |
| 1.3 | Communication and listening | Who finds it easy to be heard by me? | Offer more than one way to contribute and check understanding | One changed meeting or handoff |
| 1.4 | Accountability and ethics | Accountable to whom, and who can tell me I am wrong? | Describe a mistake and its effect without defending it | An account of one repair |
| 1.5 | Influence without a title | Whose ideas get credited, and can I change that? | Bring an overlooked contribution into a decision | One act of amplification |
| 1.6 | Planning your development | What opportunity can I reach, and what is in the way? | Complete a development map and name the support needed | The map and one conversation |

Module 1.1 uses the six questions from the Emerging Leaders Institute application as prompts, so a participant who later applies has already thought them through.

### 9.2 Pathway 2: Leading a program

**For:** program leads. **Competencies:** Leading Others; Getting Work Done; Leading Outside the Organization.
**New to this:** leading through influence and evidence. **Experienced:** asking whose agreement you have learned to seek first.

| # | Topic | Equity question | The participant can | Application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | Systems thinking | Where does this process lose people, and which people? | Map a process and mark where access fails | A process map |
| 2.2 | Leading without formal authority | Whose agreement do I seek first, and whose last? | Identify the decision owner and the people affected before proposing change | A stakeholder map |
| 2.3 | Stakeholder and community partnership | Can this engagement change the decision? | Say what is open to influence and how a response returns | An engagement plan |
| 2.4 | Policy, forms, and notices | Who can use this as written? | Review a document for plain language and access | One revised document |
| 2.5 | Using evidence | What does the total hide? | Distinguish delivery, application, and benefit evidence | A measurement note |
| 2.6 | Advising a decision owner | Have I shown the tradeoffs, including who bears them? | Prepare an options brief with a way to learn from the decision | An options brief |

### 9.3 Pathway 3: Leading a team

**For:** supervisors. **Competencies:** Leading Others; Leading Self.
**New to this:** the shift from doing the work to creating conditions for others. **Experienced:** noticing which staff your style serves best.

| # | Topic | Equity question | The participant can | Application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | Hiring and welcoming | What does the work require, and what is only familiar? | Tie each criterion to a duty and separate evidence from impression | A posting or interview guide reviewed with HR |
| 3.2 | Psychological safety and team culture | Who pays a price for disagreeing here? | Describe the team's default and one routine that would change it | One changed routine |
| 3.3 | Coaching and feedback | Is my feedback about the work or a style I prefer? | Give feedback tied to the work and ask what conditions help | One coaching conversation |
| 3.4 | Delegation and opportunity | Who gets the stretch work? | Make one opportunity visible with criteria and support | An open opportunity |
| 3.5 | Conflict and repair | Whose comfort is the resolution protecting? | Tell a matter for conversation from one for a formal process | A prepared conversation |
| 3.6 | Workload, well-being, and staying | What would make it easier to stay and do good work? | Hold a stay conversation, agree what is recorded, respond to one concern | One response returned |

### 9.4 Pathway 4: Leading across teams

**For:** managers, including deputy and assistant directors. **Competencies:** Leading the Organization; Leading Others.
**New to this:** leading through other leaders. **Experienced:** examining what your decisions cost the people furthest from you.

| # | Topic | Equity question | The participant can | Application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4.1 | Strategy and priorities | Who benefits first from what we chose to do first? | Show how a priority affects different groups | An annotated priority list |
| 4.2 | Resourcing and budget | Where does a cut or a delay land? | Run an equity analysis on a resourcing choice | An equity analysis |
| 4.3 | Leading change | Who is asked to adapt, and who is not? | Plan a change that names what is open to influence | A change plan with a report-back |
| 4.4 | Developing supervisors | Do my supervisors get the support I expect them to give? | Review how development is offered across teams | A cross-team review |
| 4.5 | Accessibility as a management duty | Is access built in, or requested each time? | Find where access depends on a person asking | One built-in change |
| 4.6 | Advising upward and deciding | What did I leave out because it was uncomfortable? | Use someone else's options brief and explain the decision back | A decision explained to those affected |

### 9.5 Pathway 5: Leading the organization

**For:** directors. **Competencies:** Leading the Organization; Leading Outside the Organization.
**New to this:** holding the whole unit's culture. **Experienced:** asking what will remain when you move on.

| # | Topic | Equity question | The participant can | Application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1 | Vision and culture | What do we reward in practice? | Compare stated values with what is recognized and resourced | A values-and-practice comparison |
| 5.2 | Governance and accountability | Who can hold us to account, and how do they reach us? | Describe how a concern reaches a decision and a response returns | A reach-and-return map |
| 5.3 | Partnership beyond the unit | Whose terms is the partnership on? | Name what each side decides before entering | A partnership preparation note |
| 5.4 | Removing a structural barrier | Which rule produces the unequal result? | Take one barrier through its proper route | One barrier addressed officially |
| 5.5 | Continuity and succession | Who has a fair chance to learn this work? | Review a critical function without naming a successor | A function-level continuity review |
| 5.6 | Stewardship | What will remain when I move on? | Write a leadership philosophy and a private legacy reflection | A philosophy statement |

## 10. Anatomy of a module, with a worked example

### 10.1 What every module contains

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Title and equity question | The conventional topic and the question that reworks it |
| State competency | One or two |
| Objectives | Three to five, each observable |
| Start here lesson | Idea, scenario from the setting, worked example. 10 to 20 minutes |
| Go further lesson | For experienced leaders: examines an established habit |
| On the continuum | The topic at Minimization, Acceptance, and Adaptation, described as practice |
| Reflection | What? So what? Now what? with one prompt specific to the module |
| Application | One thing to try, in three sizes |
| What to notice | Evidence the participant can gather themselves, with its limits |
| A conversation, if wanted | Which guide fits and what to bring |
| Go deeper | Related lessons, tools, original sources, optional outside courses |
| Where authority sits | The office or process to involve before acting, where relevant |

### 10.2 Worked example: module 3.4, Delegation and opportunity

**Equity question.** Who gets the stretch work?

**State competency.** Leading Others; Getting Work Done.

**Objectives.** After this module a participant can:

1. List the stretch assignments they handed out in the last six months and to whom.
2. Explain how "readiness" can reflect who was given earlier chances.
3. Write criteria for one opportunity that tie to the work itself.
4. Offer that opportunity so that anyone interested can see it and say so.
5. Describe what support made it possible for someone to take it up.

**Start here.** Delegation is a development decision as well as a workload decision. The lesson separates three things a supervisor usually blends: who can do the task now, who would grow from it, and who was asked. A scenario from the setting shows a supervisor who always turns to the same two people because it is faster.

**Go further.** For an experienced supervisor: look at the pattern across years. Whose careers moved because of work you gave them? Who left without having been offered any?

**On the continuum.**

| Orientation | What it sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| Minimization | "I give work to whoever is ready. It has nothing to do with background." |
| Acceptance | "Readiness partly reflects who I gave chances to before, and how comfortable I am with how someone works." |
| Adaptation | "I post the opportunity, state the criteria, and arrange the support, so more than the usual people can take it." |

**Reflection prompt.** Think of the last stretch task you handed out. What made you choose that person? What would you have needed to believe to choose someone else?

**Application, in three sizes.**

| Size | What to try |
| --- | --- |
| Small | Before your next assignment, write down who you would normally ask and one person you have never asked |
| Medium | Make one opportunity visible to the whole team with criteria and the support on offer |
| Larger | With your manager, agree how development opportunities are announced across the unit |

**What to notice.** Who expressed interest. Who took it up. What support they used. This is the participant's own observation of one opportunity. It does not show a pattern across the organization, and it is not a record about any colleague.

**A conversation, if wanted.** The sponsor conversation guide: bring the criteria and ask what you have missed.

**Where authority sits.** Assignment and classification rules, labor agreements, and accommodation processes stay with HR and labor relations. Check before changing how work is assigned.

## 11. Reflection, application, and the closing summary

### 11.1 Six kinds of reflection

| Type | When | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short reflection | After a lesson or an application step | What? So what? Now what? |
| Return reflection | About a week and a month later | A return point the participant sets, shown on their next visit |
| Critical-moment reflection | When something advanced or undermined access or inclusion | Observation, interpretation, evidence |
| After-action review | After a project or stretch task | Intended, happened, why, keep, change |
| Pathway reflection | When the participant decides they are done for now | A summary in their own words |
| Legacy reflection | Pathway 5 | Private and self-guided |

Templates remind the participant to leave out names, case details, and anything about another person's health, disability, or personnel matters.

### 11.2 Application in three sizes

| Size | Typical pathways | What it involves | Evidence the participant gathers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal or team practice | 1 and 3 | One changed behavior or routine | A before-and-after observation |
| Program or cross-team project | 2 and 4 | A change across a process or several teams | A stakeholder map, the decision owner, and delivery, application, and benefit evidence kept distinct |
| Organization-level action | 5 | A policy, structural, or continuity action through its official route | The change documented, and a reach-and-return account |

Application is invited and sized by the participant. A participant with little room to act can choose something small.

### 11.3 Self-review

Four questions at the end of a pathway, answered privately: Which lessons did I choose? Did I reflect honestly? Did I try at least one thing in real work? Can I describe what changed and what I would do next?

### 11.4 The closing summary

A short account in the participant's own words of what they learned, tried, and would do next. It carries the training-credit notice. The participant can keep it, bring it to a development conversation, or share it at a learning group. It is not a certificate and no one assesses it.

## 12. Learning with others

All of this is optional and arranged by the participant. The program contacts no one, assigns no one, and keeps no roster.

| Conversation | Purpose | The guide helps the participant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peer | Think aloud with someone at a similar scope | Agree what is shared and ask useful questions |
| Mentor | Perspective from someone more experienced | Bring a development map and a specific question |
| Sponsor | Access to an opportunity | Ask what opportunity fits and what support is needed |
| Leader interview | Learn how an experienced leader handled an equity tradeoff | Prepare questions and draw out a decision map |
| Feedback | Hear how a practice landed | Use four questions about the work and its effects |
| Learning from someone earlier in their career | See the organization from a different position | Listen without defending, and agree what may be acted on |
| Learning group | Reflect with others on a live challenge | Bring a question to an existing voluntary group |

The applications already have places for several of these: the mentoring and peer learning pages, the learning lab, the voluntary team, employee resource groups, and the consultant.

## 14. Roles

| Role | What they do | What they do not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participant | Chooses, learns, applies, reflects, shares if they wish | Owe anyone a record |
| Supervisor of a participant | Encourages; makes time; talks about development when invited | Monitor participation, require it, or treat it as performance information |
| Mentor, sponsor, or peer | Takes part in a conversation the participant requested | Receive anything the participant did not hand them |
| Program owner | Sets direction; curates and approves content; holds the face-to-face role | |

## 15. One program, two applications

### 15.1 What is shared and what is local

| Conversations | The guides | Local groups a participant can join |

### 15.2 The setting kit

To bring the program into its own work, a unit supplies:

1. Six to twelve fictional scenarios drawn from its real kinds of decisions.
2. Its commitments to the people it serves, for core module C6.
3. Its program or service profiles, for examples.
4. The decision owners and official processes its leaders most often need.
5. Voluntary groups and learning spaces a participant can join.
6. Its leadership titles, so each pathway says plainly who it is for.

### 15.3 How this fits the applications as built

The applications already publish in two scopes, with the division view showing everything shared plus its own content. The leadership program follows the same rule: the frame, skeleton, and shared lessons are published once, and a unit's setting is added in its own scope. One lesson is maintained once and appears wherever it is relevant.

The division application already holds most of a setting kit: its program profiles, practice scenarios, life-cycle examples, and voluntary groups. It is the natural place to build and prove the program first. Whether and when other units adopt it is theirs and the organization's to decide.

## 16. Safeguards

| Safeguard | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Voluntary | No nomination, cohort, deadline, or requirement |
| Private by default | Journals and portfolios stay on the participant's device; sharing is the participant handing over their own file |
| No personnel data | No assessed or inferred continuum position, score, rank, level, badge, or completion record attached to a person |
| Not performance information | Participation plays no part in appraisal, selection, or promotion |
| Training credit | Participation and completion do not count toward required training credits unless management, a director, or agency leadership expressly approves an exception |
| No promise of advancement | Formal programs and vacancies keep their own requirements |
| Assessment stays separate | Licensed instruments are not reproduced, scored, or replaced |
| Authority stays where it is | HR, selection, accommodation, labor relations, and policy processes are named, not bypassed |
| Tribal Nations and Native American communities | Content defers to the Office of Indian Affairs and the offices that handle tribal matters. The program writes none and says so plainly |
| Fictional scenarios | No case records, complaints, or accounts of named staff |
| Accessible | Captions, full transcripts, keyboard use, visible focus, narrow layouts, no autoplay |
| Retention | No staff records are collected by this program while retention remains undecided |
| Support | Where reflection raises something difficult, the page points to verified support |

## 18. Knowing whether the program is useful

Evidence is about the program and never about a person.

| Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Can people find a pathway and start? | Moderated task success |
| Do lessons make sense and feel respectful? | Voluntary feedback, with its source and limits |
| Do people apply something in real work? | Voluntary participant accounts describing the change, the period, and the limits |
| What is missing, unclear, or inaccessible? | Recorded gaps with an owner and a review date |
| Does it serve every audience? | Feedback invited from each of the five audiences |

There are no completion counts by person, no rankings, and no composite scores. An empty evidence set means no evidence in that record.

## 21. Open decisions and interview questions

### Decisions carried forward, with recommendations

| # | Decision | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Identification: invitation or nomination | Invitation |
| 2 | Badges and certificates | Leave out; offer the closing summary |
| 3 | Directories and matching | Defer; use conversation guides |
| 4 | Where the journal and portfolio live | On the participant's device |
| 5 | Program name | A name that is not "Leadership Learning Hub," which MMB already uses |
| 6 | Five questions as the frame; State competencies as the skeleton | Adopt both |

### Questions for the owner

1. **Depth.** Is six modules per pathway the right size, or do you picture something longer for supervisors and managers, who carry the most day-to-day leadership?
2. **Core.** Should the common core be recommended first, as written, or expected before a pathway?
3. **Experienced leaders.** Is a "go further" lesson in each module enough for experienced leaders, or do they need their own modules?
4. **Directors.** Should pathway 5 be self-paced like the rest, or does that audience need something with you in the room?
5. **Your role.** Where do you want to appear in person: an opening conversation, the learning groups, the closing summaries, or none by default?
6. **Supervisors of participants.** Do you want a short guide for supervisors on how to support a participant without monitoring them?
7. **Statewide use.** When you say statewide work, do you see other agencies using this program, and if so, should the State competency model be even more prominent than the continuum in how it is presented?
8. **Language.** The research sources use "DEAI," "equity-centered," and "anti-racist." Which terms do you want the program to use in staff copy?
9. **What is missing.** Is there a leadership topic you teach in person that does not appear in the 36 modules?
