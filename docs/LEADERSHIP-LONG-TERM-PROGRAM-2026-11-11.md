# People, Access and Culture leadership program: long-term design

November 11, 2026. Internal program and implementation guidance. This document is not staff-facing copy.

**Status.** Proposal for the owner's decision. No lesson, page, or tool described here has been written or built. Every timeframe is a planning pattern, not a schedule, a deadline, or a commitment of anyone's time. "Leadership Journey" remains a working title.

**Owner direction this document answers, November 11, 2026.**

1. The leadership program is a significant addition to the One DSD People, Access and Culture application.
2. It may use, adapt, and revise existing materials for the leadership section.
3. It is to be a long-term program, informed by more comprehensive outside research.
4. It must carry a consistent and very focused intercultural educational component.
5. The program and the applications belong to the consultant. They are used in the DHS and DSD context and are not dependent on DHS or DSD.

**What the fifth point means for the design.** This document records it as the owner's statement of ownership; it does not establish or test any legal or agency position. In design terms it sets one rule: nothing in the program may require a DHS or DSD system, office, licence, roster, or decision in order to work. DSD is the first setting the program is used in. Section 3.1 shows how that is kept true.

**How this fits the earlier documents.** The blueprint (`LEADERSHIP-PROGRAM-BLUEPRINT-2026-11-11.md`) set the frame, skeleton, core, and five pathways. This document keeps all of that and adds three things: a dedicated intercultural strand, a multi-year shape for the participant, and a multi-year plan for the program itself. The research behind it is in `LEADERSHIP-RESEARCH-COMPENDIUM-2026-11-11.md`. The existing materials it draws on are in `LEADERSHIP-EXISTING-MATERIALS-INVENTORY-2026-11-11.md`.

## 2. What the research says the design must do

Each point is drawn from the compendium, which records the source and how firmly it was verified.

| Finding | Design response |
| --- | --- |
| Leadership training spread over time transfers to work better than training delivered at once | A multi-year arc with monthly rhythm |
| Voluntary programs show stronger transfer than mandatory ones; voluntary equity training is associated with gains where mandatory training is not | Stay voluntary |
| Self-paced and virtual formats are the weakest for transfer | Every unit ends in real-work practice; conversations and learning groups are built in as real options, not afterthoughts |
| Information, demonstration, and practice together beat any one alone | Every unit has an idea, a worked scenario, and a practice |
| Supervisor support, opportunity to apply, and follow-up decide whether learning transfers | A guide for supervisors of participants; applications sized to the participant's real room to act; scheduled returns |
| Equity learning lasts when it pairs awareness with skills, runs over a significant period, and sits inside wider equity work | The intercultural strand teaches skills as well as awareness, runs for three years, and connects to the One DSD Team, Amplify, and the Equity Analysis Tool |
| Knowledge persists; attitude change fades | Quarterly returns and a yearly refresh |
| Mentoring and shared responsibility for real work sustain change more than training does | Mentoring conversations, and applications that can become real work items with a named decision owner |
| Exposure to difference alone does not build intercultural capability; guided reflection and a mentor matter most | The intercultural strand is guided: structured reflection in every unit, a learning-partner guide, and the option of working with an IDI Qualified Administrator |
| The State's own IDI guidance says moving from one orientation to the next takes 30 to 60 hours of intentional, consistent learning | The intercultural strand is sized at roughly 25 hours a year, about 75 over three years. This is an estimate of effort, never a promise of movement |
| Most public-sector frameworks reuse the same capabilities at each level, re-worded for the scope | The same intercultural practices recur in every pathway, scaled to the role |
| Programs that last build in transitions, refresh cycles, and a continuing layer | Transition guides, a yearly refresh, and continuing practice after year three |

## 3. The program's four strands

Every month and every year of the program carries all four.

| Strand | What it is | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intercultural** | A sustained, sequenced course of study in intercultural practice, organized on the IDI continuum | New in this document; built largely from existing courses |
| **Leadership** | The common core and five pathways, taught through the five equity questions | The blueprint |
| **Application** | Real work, growing from a personal practice to a project to stewardship | The blueprint, extended over three years |
| **Relationship** | Conversations with a learning partner, mentor, sponsor, experienced leader, or learning group | The blueprint, strengthened |

Reflection runs through all four.

### 3.1 Independent of any one agency

| Part of the program | Belongs to the program | Supplied by the setting it is used in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frame, intercultural strand, core, pathways, guides, tools | Yes. Written as original program content | |
| Scenarios and worked examples | A general set | The setting's own cases. For DSD: the existing program profiles and practice scenarios |
| The commitments to people served (core module C6) | The structure | The setting's content. For DSD: presuming competence, dignity of risk, self-determination |
| Policy and official sources | Linked as outside sources, never as the program's foundation | The setting's own policies and decision owners |
| Leadership competencies | The program's five scopes of leadership | A crosswalk to the setting's model. For Minnesota state service: the MMB competency model |
| Intercultural assessment | None. The strand needs no assessment | Whatever the setting or the participant arranges independently |
| Learning groups and mentoring | Conversation guides | Whatever voluntary groups the setting has |

Three consequences:

1. The State competency model, MMB programs, the DHS Equity Policy, and the State Equity Analysis Tool are treated as a **crosswalk and outside references** for this setting. The program's own structure stands without them.
2. The intercultural strand works with no IDI assessment at all. Where a participant has one, it helps them choose; where they do not, nothing is lost.
3. The DSD setting content already in the application is one setting kit. Another organization would supply its own.

**How the intercultural strand and the equity frame differ.** The frame is a method: five questions put to every leadership topic. The intercultural strand is content: what culture is, how one's own culture operates, how difference is perceived and responded to, and how a person and a system learn to adapt. A participant meets the frame inside every leadership module, and studies the intercultural strand in its own right, month after month.

## 4. The intercultural strand

### 4.1 Purpose

To build intercultural fluency: the capability to shift cultural perspective and adapt behavior appropriately to cultural differences and commonalities. That is the State's own description of what the IDI measures, in MMB's IDI Program Guidelines.

The organization as a whole sits near Minimization. The strand's main work is therefore the passage from Minimization to Acceptance and from Acceptance to Adaptation, with honest attention to Denial and Polarization before it and Integration after.

### 4.2 Design commitments

1. **Consistent.** One intercultural unit every month of the program, in every pathway, for every audience.
2. **Focused.** The strand has its own sequence, objectives, and practices. It is not folded into leadership topics and it is not optional relative to them.
3. **Developmental.** Units follow the continuum in order, because each orientation has its own task.
4. **Guided.** Every unit carries structured reflection, and the strand offers a learning-partner guide, learning-group sessions, and the option of professional IDI support.
5. **Skills as well as awareness.** Each unit ends in something to do.
6. **About practice, never about ranking people.** The continuum describes practices, teams, and systems. No one is placed on it by the program.
7. **Whole-person.** Examining one's own culture is the first third of the strand, and it applies with particular force to dominant-culture staff.
8. **Context is not prediction.** Cultural frameworks and community knowledge prepare better questions. They never say what an individual believes, needs, or will do.

### 4.3 The developmental task at each orientation

Described as practice, in the program's own words.

| Orientation | What it looks like | The work that moves practice forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Denial | Difference goes unnoticed or is avoided | Notice difference, starting with one's own culture |
| Polarization | Difference is judged, as "ours is better" or "theirs is better" | Lower the judgment; find real commonality; see both forms in oneself |
| Minimization | Commonality is stressed; one's own norm passes as universal | Deepen cultural self-awareness; learn what difference actually consists of; see who is doing the adapting |
| Acceptance | Difference is understood as real and meaningful | Build skill in adapting; work through what to do when values conflict |
| Adaptation | Perspective and behavior shift to fit the context | Widen the range; bridge between perspectives; change processes, not only oneself |
| Integration | Equity and adaptation are how the unit works | Sustain it through change in leaders; teach and mentor others |

### 4.4 Five intercultural practices

These five practices recur in every unit and every pathway. They are the strand's skills.

| Practice | What the participant does |
| --- | --- |
| **Know your own culture** | Name the norms, values, and habits they bring, and where they learned them |
| **Separate observation from interpretation** | Describe what happened before deciding what it means or whether it is good |
| **Shift perspective** | Work out how the same situation is experienced from another position |
| **Adapt behavior** | Change how they communicate, meet, decide, or resolve conflict, and check that it helped |
| **Bridge** | Help people and processes work across difference, including changing the process itself |

### 4.5 Three lenses

Every unit looks through three lenses in turn: **in yourself, in your team, in the system.** A participant early in their leadership spends most time on the first; a director spends most on the third. All three appear every time.

### 4.6 The sequence

Twenty-four units across three years: eight a year, with a return month each quarter. A participant can enter at any unit; the order is a recommendation.

**Year one: Ground.** From noticing difference to seeing one's own norm. Denial, Polarization, and Minimization.

| # | Unit | The participant can | Existing material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I-1 | Culture, and your own | Describe three norms of their own culture and where each was learned | `div-f04-cultural-values`; `cq-*` foundations |
| I-2 | The continuum | Describe each orientation as practice, without placing anyone on it | `intercultural-competence-without-a-score`; six `idi-*` courses |
| I-3 | Noticing Denial | Find one place where difference goes unnoticed in their own work | `idi-denial` |
| I-4 | Noticing Polarization, in both directions | Recognize an us-and-them judgment of their own, including the reversed form | `idi-polarization`; `from-noticing-to-shifting` |
| I-5 | What Minimization does, and why it feels fair | Explain how "I treat everyone the same" can produce unequal results | `idi-minimization`; `what-minimization-does-in-dhs-work` |
| I-6 | Observation, interpretation, evaluation | Separate the three in a recent interaction | Focus "Begin with curiosity"; life-cycle stage, interviewing |
| I-7 | Whose norm is the norm | Name who adapts to whom in one routine, and at what cost | `div-f08-ethnocentrism`; `div-a02-whiteness-institutional-norm`; `teams-in-minimization` |
| I-8 | Curiosity and cultural humility as habits | Ask a respectful question where they would once have assumed | Focus "Make room for different experiences" |

**Year two: Practice.** From understanding difference to adapting. Acceptance and Adaptation.

| # | Unit | The participant can | Existing material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I-9 | Acceptance: difference is real | Describe a difference that matters in their work without ranking it | `idi-acceptance`; `acceptance-in-dhs-work` |
| I-10 | Cultural value patterns, held as questions | Use a value framework to form a question, and say why it predicts nothing about an individual | `cq-*` cluster courses |
| I-11 | Communication styles | Adapt how they communicate to a stated need and check it worked | Focus "Adapt how you communicate"; `ipe-06-accessible-communication` |
| I-12 | Conflict styles | Recognize their own conflict style and one unlike it | `intercultural-conflict-styles` |
| I-13 | Shifting perspective | Give an account of a disagreement from the other position that the other person would accept | `critical-incidents-in-the-work` |
| I-14 | Adapting without losing yourself | Distinguish adapting from pretending, and name who carries the burden of constant adjustment | `idi-adaptation`; `div-a05-racial-battle-fatigue` |
| I-15 | When values conflict | Work through a case where respect for difference meets a commitment that cannot bend | `ipe-27-ethical-decision-making` |
| I-16 | Disability culture and language access | Explain disability as culture and identity, and adapt one communication for access | `ipe-02-disability-culture-justice`; `ipe-10-language-access` |

**Year three: Extend.** From personal adaptation to teams and systems. Adaptation and Integration.

| # | Unit | The participant can | Existing material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I-17 | Whose culture is the team's culture | Describe their team's unwritten norms and who they fit | `teams-in-minimization`; `ipe-29-reflective-team` |
| I-18 | Feedback and coaching across difference | Give feedback about the work that does not depend on a preferred style | `div-i07-gender-double-binds`; `div-i10-receiving-feedback` |
| I-19 | Assessing people fairly across difference | Separate job-related evidence from cultural impression in a selection decision | Life-cycle stage, interviewing; scenario `dsd-hiring-panel` |
| I-20 | Bridging | Help two people or teams understand each other's reasoning | `ipe-36-leading-difficult-dialogue`; `ipe-26-power-conflict-repair` |
| I-21 | Adapting the process, not only yourself | Change one process so fewer people have to work around it | `ipe-12-policy-writing-equity-lens`; scenario `dsd-accessible-form` |
| I-22 | Community context as a question | Use a community brief to prepare questions, and state its limits | Minnesota Communities; `cq-*` community profiles |
| I-23 | Integration: when equity is how we work | Describe what would have to be true for a practice to survive a change of leader | `idi-integration`; `culture-without-theater` |
| I-24 | Teaching and mentoring across difference | Help someone else examine a default without shaming them | `facilitators-guide-equity-inclusion-curriculum` |

Unit I-22 uses community briefs. The Native American and Tribal Nations briefs are outside this program's authorship and defer to the Office of Indian Affairs and the DHS offices that handle tribal matters. The unit says so plainly and does not draw on them.

### 4.7 Anatomy of an intercultural unit

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Notice | A short scenario from DSD work in which the unit's idea is visible |
| Understand | The idea, with where it sits on the continuum |
| Three lenses | In yourself, in your team, in the system |
| Practice | One of the five intercultural practices, tried in real work |
| Reflect | What? So what? Now what? and one prompt specific to the unit |
| With someone | A question to take to a learning partner or group, if wanted |
| Return | A return point about a month later |

Each unit has three to five observable objectives, written when the unit is authored.

### 4.8 Guided support

Research on intercultural development is consistent that guidance matters more than exposure. The strand offers four kinds, all optional.

| Support | What it is | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structured reflection | Built into every unit | Private, on the participant's device |
| Intercultural learning partner | A guide for two colleagues who agree to work through units together | The participants arrange it |
| Learning-group sessions | Intercultural topics brought to the existing Learning Lab | Voluntary; no schedule is published until confirmed |
| The IDI with a Qualified Administrator | A participant who wants an assessment, a debrief, and an Individual Development Plan can seek one through the route the agency offers | Arm's-length and professionally administered. The program never administers, scores, stores, or sees a result |

**How the strand relates to the State's IDI approach.** MMB's IDI Program Guidelines list four components an agency needs: someone to lead, a plan, a team of Qualified Administrators, and "ongoing learning and development opportunities for IDI participants." This strand can serve as that fourth component wherever an agency runs an IDI program, and it does not depend on one. A participant with a development plan can use its goals to choose units. A participant without an assessment has exactly the same access.

One caution from the same State guidance: it suggests agencies may discuss cultural-development progress in performance management. This program does not. No assessed or inferred position becomes personnel data here.

### 4.9 The strand in each pathway

The same five practices, scaled to the role.

| Pathway | Where the intercultural strand shows up in the work |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Leading yourself | Their own interactions: what they notice, assume, and adapt |
| 2. Leading a program | Who a process works for; whose perspective shaped a policy or notice |
| 3. Leading a team | Team norms, hiring, feedback, opportunity, and conflict across difference |
| 4. Leading across teams | Which teams and staff carry the adapting; how resources and change land across difference |
| 5. Leading the division | What the division's culture rewards; whether adaptation survives a change of leader |

## 5. The participant's long-term journey

### 5.1 The arc

A typical path, for someone who wants one. Anyone can enter anywhere, move at any pace, and stop.

| | Year one: Ground | Year two: Practice | Year three: Extend | After: Sustain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intercultural** | Units I-1 to I-8 | Units I-9 to I-16 | Units I-17 to I-24 | Quarterly returns; new units as they are added |
| **Leadership** | Common core, then the "start here" lessons of one pathway | The "go further" lessons of that pathway | A second pathway, or the next scope of work | Yearly refresh; transition guides when a role changes |
| **Application** | Personal and team practices | One project, with a decision owner and evidence | A stewardship action: an open opportunity, a barrier, or continuity | Ongoing practice |
| **Relationship** | A learning partner; one mentoring conversation | A leader interview; a feedback conversation; a sponsor conversation | Mentoring someone else; contributing to a learning group | Mentoring; contributing |
| **Closing** | A year summary in their own words | A project account | A leadership philosophy | |

### 5.2 A month in the program

| Week | What happens | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One | An intercultural unit: notice, understand, three lenses | 30 minutes |
| Two | A leadership module: lesson and reflection | 30 minutes |
| Three | Application in real work | 60 to 90 minutes, inside the job |
| Four | Reflection, and a conversation if wanted | 20 to 50 minutes |

In four months of each year, week one is a **return** instead of a new unit: revisit an earlier unit, look at what happened since, and keep, change, or stop.

### 5.3 Effort

| Strand | Estimate per year |
| --- | --- |
| Intercultural: 8 units, 4 returns, practice, and conversation | About 25 hours |
| Leadership: 12 modules with reflection and application | About 25 hours |
| **Total** | **About 50 hours a year, roughly an hour a week** |

Much of the application time is the participant's ordinary work done differently. These figures are estimates and have not been tested with DSD staff. They are sized against the State's guidance that moving between orientations takes 30 to 60 hours of intentional learning. The program does not measure or promise movement.

### 5.4 Transitions

Public-sector programs consistently put learning at the moment a role changes. The program offers a short guide for each transition, to use in the first year of a new role:

| Transition | The guide helps with |
| --- | --- |
| Into leading a program | Leading through influence; finding the decision owner |
| Into supervising | The shift from doing the work to creating conditions; the first one-to-ones; where required supervisor training sits |
| Into managing | Leading through other leaders; the first resourcing decision |
| Into directing | Holding the whole division's culture; the first hundred days of listening |

Each guide points to the pathway modules that matter most in the first year and to the formal State program for that role. Required training such as Supervisor Core and Manager Core is separate and is never replaced.

### 5.5 Sustaining after year three

| Element | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Quarterly returns | A prompt to revisit a practice and look at what happened |
| Yearly refresh | New cases and revised lessons each year |
| Leadership conversations | A recorded series on real leadership tradeoffs. Authoring belongs to the owner, the Chief of Staff, and the agents; staff play them |
| Contribution | A participant can suggest a scenario, bring a question to a learning group, or mentor someone, if they choose |
| A second or third pathway | For a new role or a wider view |

### 5.6 Marking progress without credentials

A participant marks their own milestones on their own device: a year summary, a project account, a leadership philosophy. Each can be exported and carries the training-credit notice. There are no badges, certificates, levels, or completion records held by the program.

## 6. The conditions that make it work

Research is clear that content alone does not change practice. Four conditions sit beside the curriculum.

| Condition | What the program provides | What it depends on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supervisor support | A short guide: how to encourage a participant, make room for practice, and talk about development when invited, without monitoring or requiring anything | Supervisors choosing to use it |
| Opportunity to apply | Applications in three sizes, so anyone has something within their reach | The participant's real room to act |
| Visible leadership | Division leaders taking part themselves, and saying so if they wish | Leaders' own choice |
| Real work | A project can become a One DSD Team work item, with its question, decision owner, and outcome recorded as work | The Team's and the decision owner's agreement |

The last row matters most. Studies of organizations find that shared responsibility for real work and mentoring are associated with lasting change where training alone is not. The application already has the Team workspace, Amplify mentoring, and the Learning Lab. The leadership program connects to them; it does not duplicate them.

## 7. Using, adapting, and revising existing materials

The full map is in the inventory. In summary:

| Treatment | Leadership modules | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use | 11 | Link the existing course, path, tool, or scenario as it is |
| Adapt | 17 | Select lessons, add a leadership framing, and rewrite the closing practice |
| Revise | 4 | Assemble a new module from several existing sources |
| New | 4 | Write from little: purpose and values; strategy and priorities; vision and culture; stewardship |

For the intercultural strand, all 24 units have existing material to start from. Integration (unit I-23) has the least.

**Rules for adapting.**

1. Original institutional source text is preserved. Leadership framing is added alongside it.
2. Recovered course packs keep their source history; adapted versions are new, traceable program-authored content.
3. One lesson is maintained once and shown wherever it belongs. A lesson used in a pathway is the same lesson a person finds in Learning.
4. Templated closing sentences, found on every lesson of the 45 recovered program-authored courses, are rewritten when a lesson is placed in a pathway.
5. Material touching Native American communities or Tribal Nations is left out and deferred, as listed in the inventory.
6. Every adapted lesson meets the lesson standard: three to five observable objectives, a calling-in approach, a DSD example.
7. Each course appears as a tile with its original title, a short description, and a relevant photograph.

## 9. Knowing whether it is useful, over years

Evidence is about the program, never about a person.

| Method | What it gives | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moderated sessions | Whether people can find, use, and understand the program | A small number of volunteers |
| Voluntary participant accounts | What someone tried, over what period, and what they observed | Self-reported; the participant chooses to share |
| Most significant change stories | Opt-in accounts of a change, read together by a panel of colleagues | Not a measure of how common the change is |
| Contribution reasoning | A stated chain from the program to a change, tested against other explanations | Supports "contributed to," never "caused" |
| Recorded gaps | What was inaccessible, unclear, or missing, with an owner and a review date | Depends on people reporting |

Any collection of staff responses, even anonymous, waits for the retention decision the PRD records as open. No completion counts by person, no rankings, and no scores are kept. The program does not claim movement along the continuum for any person or for the division.

## 10. Standing rules that still apply

The safeguards in the outline and blueprint apply unchanged: voluntary; private by default; no personnel data; not performance information; no training credit without an express exception; no promise of advancement; licensed assessment kept separate; official authority left where it is; deference on Native American and Tribal content; fictional scenarios; accessibility; no staff records while retention is undecided.

Two points this document adds:

1. **The program stays voluntary even though one study found mandatory leadership training produced stronger organization-level results.** The same study found voluntary training transferred better to individual practice, and research on equity training favors voluntary participation. The PRD also requires it.
2. **The intercultural strand's size is not a quota.** The hours are an estimate to help a participant plan. Nothing checks them.

### Questions

1. **The IDI.** Are you an IDI Qualified Administrator, and do you want the program to point participants toward you, toward an agency route, or toward neither?
2. **Your presence.** Research says guidance matters most for intercultural growth. Where do you want to be the guide in person: learning-group sessions, year-end conversations, something else?
3. **Emphasis.** The strand gives a third of its time each to Ground, Practice, and Extend. Since staff sit near Minimization, should more of it go to the Minimization-to-Acceptance passage?
4. **Three years.** Is three years the right length for the typical arc, or do you picture two, or an open-ended practice?
5. **Leadership conversations.** Do you want a recorded series as the continuing layer, and would you want to be in it?
6. **Portability.** Should the program's own name and pages avoid "DSD" entirely, with DSD appearing only in the setting content?
7. **Language.** The State's IDI guidance says "intercultural fluency" and "cultural fluency." The application says "intercultural." Which do you want staff to read?
